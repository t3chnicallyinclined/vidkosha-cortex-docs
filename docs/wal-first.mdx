## WAL in plain English

A **WAL (write-ahead log)** is an append-only “receipt tape” of everything that happened.

Instead of updating lots of databases in-place and hoping they stay consistent, you **first write the fact to the log**. Then any number of systems can replay that log to rebuild indexes, caches, and views.

“WAL-first” means one thing: **the only thing you must never lose is the append-only event stream**. Everything else (indexes, graphs, caches, views, derived tables) is replaceable.

For operators, that translates to a very practical posture:

- If JetStream is healthy and you’re publishing correctly, the system can be rebuilt.
- If JetStream is unhealthy or you are “publishing” into a void, you are not operating the protocol.

## Why JetStream is the right WAL here

JetStream is a good fit because it’s both:

- a **durable log** (replayable, auditable), and
- a **high-throughput fanout fabric** (many consumers, many lanes).

In a DRAG-style network, you want *many* consumers to see the same facts:

- projection/index builders
- marketplace metering
- archival pack builders
- monitoring and integrity checkers

This is exactly the “Netflix/gaming-class” pattern: a durable stream feeding many independent services.

## The scaling trick: bounded hot truth + paid cold history

The easiest way to keep a global event fabric economically scalable is to **bound the hot window**.

Example rule:

- JetStream retains the raw WAL for $T$ days (e.g. 7d).
- Anything older is only available if someone paid to keep it as archival packs.

Why this matters:

- Hot storage cost becomes predictable.
- Freshness becomes a real guarantee (“last 7 days is always fast”).
- Long-term memory becomes a market instead of a hidden liability.

## Operator responsibilities (what you actually need to get right)

- **Idempotency:** replay will happen; your consumers must tolerate duplicates.
- **Schema versioning:** envelopes evolve; plan for forward/back compatibility.
- **Lag is a KPI:** consumer lag is capacity pressure; treat it like a queue depth.
- **Failure visibility:** if JetStream is down, ingestion should fail loudly (no silent drops).

## Failure modes to design for

- **Slow consumers:** lag increases; you scale consumers horizontally.
- **Reprocessing:** deploy a fixed projection, rewind, rebuild.
- **Retention boundary:** after $T$, raw events disappear; if you need history, it must be promoted into a pack (or sponsored).

## How this ties into training / accelerators

Training workflows are expensive and sensitive to reproducibility. WAL-first helps because:

- jobs reference **dataset packs** by manifest hash,
- preprocessing/tool steps are **deterministic and auditable**, and
- the network can replay job streams to verify accounting and outcomes.
