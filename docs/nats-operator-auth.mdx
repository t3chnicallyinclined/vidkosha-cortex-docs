This is the operator-side security model we target: **fast rotation, least privilege, and “deny by default” subject access**.

The goal isn’t academic security — it’s operational safety at scale.

## Why this matters (operator + investor view)

At “Netflix/gaming-class” scale you will have:

- many operators,
- many lanes (ingest, packs, retrieval, tools, metering), and
- many tenants/topics.

So the security boundary has to be cheap to enforce and easy to rotate. Subject allowlists are the simplest durable primitive: they turn multi-tenant isolation into configuration.

## Minimal posture

- **NATS identity via nkeys** for operators.
- **Least-privilege permissions** (publish/subscribe allowlists), scoped to exactly what an operator provides.
- **Easy revocation/rotation**: if you suspect compromise, you rotate keys and cut the operator out of routing.

This is intentionally compatible with the marketplace: an operator listing can bind “who you are” (keys) to “what you’re allowed to touch” (subjects) to “what you get paid for” (usage events).

## Practical policy design

- Separate lanes:
	- ingest facts vs mission/work requests vs metering/settlement
- Use subject patterns to enforce boundaries:
	- tenant/topic scoped subjects
	- dedicated lanes for privileged producers (e.g. payout batching)
- Default to *read-only* when possible (operators that serve packs may only subscribe to request subjects and publish responses + usage events).

## Key separation (transport vs economics)

If the chain lane exists, treat it as **off by default** and keep keys conceptually separate:

- one identity for NATS transport permissions
- a separate identity for economic settlement (chain signatures)

This keeps “can I publish to a subject” independent from “can I move money”.

## Training / accelerator lane isolation

Training jobs can include sensitive data and expensive compute. Subject ACLs make it possible to:

- isolate training job request lanes
- restrict dataset pack access to authorized providers
- route evaluation and artifact publication through tightly scoped subjects
