A **pack** is a sealed, integrity-checked bundle of knowledge that the network can store, serve, and charge for.

If the WAL (JetStream) is the **hot, replayable stream of facts**, packs are the **cold, durable, queryable artifacts**.

## Why packs exist

Most RAG systems treat “history” as an unbounded hidden cost. Packs turn history into a **product**:

- bounded hot window (fast/cheap)
- explicit cold history (paid storage + paid retrieval)

That’s what makes the operator economy sustainable.

## What’s inside a pack

At minimum, a useful pack contains:

- **normalized documents** (content + metadata + provenance)
- **chunk map** (chunk IDs and offsets for citation)
- **embeddings** (or a deterministic recipe pinned by model/version)
- **index hints** (topic tags, time range, source identifiers)
- **integrity manifest** (hashes/Merkle root over the pack contents)

A pack is typically keyed by something like:

- `(topic, source, time_range, schema_version, embedding_recipe_version)`

## What makes packs different from “a file on IPFS”

A pack is designed to be **served as a low-latency service** with explicit expectations:

- operators advertise prices and SLO tiers
- routing picks providers by policy (price/SLO/geo), not opportunistic peer discovery
- clients can verify integrity via the manifest

This is how the cold path stays usable for interactive AI workflows.

## How packs are created

Conceptually:

1. Ingest facts into the WAL.
2. Projection operators normalize/chunk/embed and maintain hot indices.
3. Periodically seal a time slice (or curated slice) into a pack with a manifest.
4. Storage operators retain packs and serve them on demand.
5. Retrieval events are metered and paid.

## What packs enable (high-value use cases)

- **Paid historical retrieval:** “pull me the 2023–2024 history for topic X”.
- **Retention tiers:** 7d / 30d / 90d / 1y tiers priced by storage + replication + SLO.
- **Time-travel RAG:** spin up “RAG as-of date $D$” by rehydrating projections from packs.
- **Dataset packs:** dataset packs referenced by manifest for reproducible offline workflows.

## Packs by example (novice-friendly)

Think of a pack like a **“semantic, searchable folder”** that covers a topic and a time window.

As an operator, your value is:

- you **ingest + normalize** a stream or dataset,
- you **seal it** into a pack with an integrity manifest,
- and you **serve it reliably** (low latency, high uptime) so people can semantic-search it.

Here are concrete pack ideas people actually want to query:

### 1) “Hashtag / topic stream” packs (social + communities)

Example: “AI safety” or “Solana dev” mentions over the last week/month.

- Source: any social stream you are legally allowed to ingest (APIs/exports; obey ToS/licensing).
- Pack contents: posts, authors (where permitted), timestamps, links, extracted entities, embeddings.
- Buyers want: “what changed today?”, “top emerging subtopics”, “who is driving the conversation?”.

### 2) News feed packs (fresh + searchable)

Example: “Energy policy news (US/EU), last 30 days”.

- Source: RSS feeds, press releases, public newsroom pages (respect terms).
- Pack contents: normalized articles, source metadata, topic tags, embeddings.
- Buyers want: “summarize this week”, “find all mentions of company X + regulation Y”.

### 3) Research packs (papers + preprints)

Example: “All arXiv papers in cs.LG this week” or “papers mentioning ‘diffusion policy’ in 2024”.

- Source: open indexes where allowed (metadata + PDFs where licensed).
- Pack contents: paper metadata, abstract, sections/chunks, citations graph hints, embeddings.
- Buyers want: “what’s new?”, “find prior art”, “map a research area”.

### 4) Market + macro packs (public, queryable)

Example: “FRED macro series last 10 years”, “SEC EDGAR filings for sector X in 2024”.

- Source: open datasets (FRED, EDGAR, World Bank, etc.).
- Pack contents: normalized time series + filings text chunks + embeddings.
- Buyers want: “backtest prompts”, “explain macro regimes”, “pull all risk factors mentioning X”.

### 5) “Open knowledge base” packs (standards + docs)

Example: “RFCs for HTTP/QUIC”, “Kubernetes docs snapshots per release”.

- Source: open docs / permissively licensed content.
- Pack contents: normalized docs, version tags, changelog deltas, embeddings.
- Buyers want: “search across versions”, “diff what changed between releases”.

### 6) Local / municipal open-data packs

Example: “NYC 311 complaints (last 90 days)”, “crime + zoning + permits by neighborhood”.

- Source: city open-data portals.
- Pack contents: normalized records, geo tags, time windows, embeddings for free-text fields.
- Buyers want: “spot trends”, “answer civic questions with citations”.

### 7) Security intelligence packs

Example: “CVE + advisories last 30 days” or “vendor advisories for product family X”.

- Source: public advisories, CVE feeds.
- Pack contents: normalized advisories, affected versions, links to patches, embeddings.
- Buyers want: “what affects me?”, “generate remediation plans with grounding”.

### 8) “Curated media transcript” packs

Example: “Top 200 podcasts in fintech (last 60 days)” or “specific YouTube channels by topic”.

- Source: transcripts where permitted.
- Pack contents: transcript chunks with timestamps, speaker tags (if available), embeddings.
- Buyers want: “find the 20 seconds where they discussed X”, “build clip packs”.

## A simple mental model for selling packs

- You are selling **coverage** (topic/time range), **quality** (cleaning/chunking/recipe pins), and **serving reliability** (latency/SLO).
- Packs become more valuable when you add: better metadata, better chunking, pinned embedding recipes, and higher replication/uptime tiers.
